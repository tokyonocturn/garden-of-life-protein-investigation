# 03 — Ingredient database

Every ingredient that appears in a Garden of Life organic plant protein, what it does, what the evidence says, and what to watch for. Amounts are only disclosed where the label discloses them; "blend" ingredients are listed in descending order by weight but without grams.

## 3.1 Protein sources

| Ingredient | Function | Protein quality note | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic pea protein** | Primary protein; listed first in Raw Organic Protein, Meal, Fit, SPORT, Protein & Greens | Lysine-rich, low in methionine/cysteine. Pea protein concentrate PDCAAS ≈0.89, DIAAS ≈0.82; a 2022 human ileal study measured pea isolate DIAAS 1.00, showing method dependence | Established as a good plant protein; inferior to whey on DIAAS in most assays |
| **Organic sprouted brown rice protein** | Second protein; complements pea's low methionine | Lysine-limited (PDCAAS ≈0.42, DIAAS ≈0.37 as an isolate), but high in methionine. Rice also accumulates **inorganic arsenic and cadmium** from soil and water | Established; the metal accumulation is the concern |
| **Organic sprouted navy bean / lentil / garbanzo** | Minor proteins and fiber in SPORT and Meal | Legume proteins, lysine-rich | Plausible but unquantified |
| **Organic cranberry seed protein** | Minor protein in SPORT | Little independent quality data | Marketing-forward, evidence thin |
| **Organic chia protein** | Minor protein, fiber, fat | Complete-ish but low density | Plausible |
| **Sprouted amaranth, buckwheat, millet, quinoa, adzuki, flax, sunflower, pumpkin, sesame** | "14 sprouts" story; trace protein, fiber, minerals | Contribute little protein at these amounts; sprouting reduces phytate and improves mineral availability modestly | The sprouting benefit is real but small at supplement doses |
| **Cracked-wall chlorella** | Micronutrients, color, possible vitamin D2 | Cell-wall cracking improves digestibility | Amount undisclosed; effect unquantified |

**The blend logic is sound in principle.** Pea is low in sulfur amino acids; rice is low in lysine. Together they cover each other's gaps, which is why the flagship's amino acid score (0.91, limited by Met+Cys) is far better than rice alone (0.42) and close to pea alone (0.89). Whether the actual ratio achieves this depends on the undisclosed pea:rice proportion and on batch variation.

## 3.2 Sweeteners

| Ingredient | Where | Function | Dose in product | Evidence and caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic erythritol** | Raw Organic Protein (vanilla, chocolate, chai), Creamy Oatmilk | Bulk sweetener, masks pea/rice bitterness | Not disclosed; typically 2–5 g per serving in this category | A 30 g oral dose acutely increased platelet aggregation and thromboxane markers in healthy volunteers (n=10). Observational cohorts associate higher plasma erythritol with MACE (HR 1.8–2.2 highest vs lowest quartile). EFSA's 2023 re-evaluation found **no demonstrated causal link** and identified diarrhoea as the most sensitive endpoint. A typical 2–3 g serving is ~10× below the intervention dose, so the practical risk is uncertain and probably small — but it is not zero and it is not "natural therefore inert" |
| **Organic stevia leaf extract** | Nearly every flavored SKU | High-intensity sweetener | Not disclosed | JECFA ADI 0–4 mg/kg body weight/day (as steviol equivalents); FDA GRAS for purified steviol glycosides. No established safety signal at food doses. Whole-leaf extract is not the same as purified Reb A; the label does not state glycoside content |
| **Organic monk fruit** | Not used in these SKUs (used by competitors) | — | — | — |

**"0 g sugar" is accurate but incomplete.** The sugar alcohols and steviol glycosides are not sugars, so the claim is legal. It says nothing about the total sweetener load, which is undisclosed.

## 3.3 Gums, fibers and thickeners

| Ingredient | Where | Function | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic guar gum** | Raw Organic Protein, SPORT chocolate | Thickener, suspending agent | 5–11.4 g/day well tolerated; 13–40 g/day causes mild-to-moderate flatulence. Amount here is likely <1 g |
| **Organic carob bean gum** | Some Raw Organic Protein labels | Thickener | Similar to guar; well tolerated at low dose |
| **Organic acacia gum** | Creamy Oatmilk | Prebiotic fiber, emulsifier | Low fermentability, generally well tolerated |
| **Organic inulin** | Creamy Oatmilk | Prebiotic fiber | Up to 10 g/day native inulin well tolerated; >10 g can cause gas and bloating. Amount undisclosed |
| **Xanthan gum** | Creamy Oatmilk | Thickener | Well tolerated at food doses; large doses have a laxative effect |
| **Organic tapioca fiber** | Meal, Fit | Fiber, bulking | Generally well tolerated |
| **Organic chia, flax meal** | Meal, Fit | Fiber, fat | Well tolerated; contribute to the 7–9 g fiber load |

**Net digestive effect:** the fiber load (5–9 g) is a genuine benefit for most people and a genuine problem for people with IBS or a sensitive gut. Inulin and the legume fibers are fermentable; the resulting gas is dose-dependent and highly individual.

## 3.4 Flavors, colors and "natural flavors"

| Ingredient | What it actually is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **"Organic vanilla flavors with other natural flavors"** | A proprietary flavor compound, legally permitted to be called natural if derived from plant/animal sources | "Natural" is a labeling category, not a safety or purity claim. The components are undisclosed; some consumers react to specific flavor chemicals |
| **Organic cocoa / cacao** | Flavor, polyphenols | Cocoa is a **cadmium accumulator** — the reason chocolate-flavored powders test higher for cadmium than vanilla in Clean Label Project's data |
| **Organic carob** | Flavor, sweetness | Legume; low risk |
| **Organic cinnamon, chai flavors** | Flavor | Cinnamon contains coumarin; at flavor doses this is negligible |
| **Sea salt** | Flavor, sodium | 150–460 mg sodium per serving depending on line |
| **Organic turmeric, tart cherry, apple, blueberry, goji** | "Recovery blend" in SPORT | Antioxidant marketing; amounts undisclosed and almost certainly below effective doses |

## 3.5 Probiotics and enzymes

| Ingredient | Dose | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|---|
| ***Bacillus subtilis* DE111** | 250 million CFU (Raw Organic Protein, Creamy Oatmilk) or 2 billion CFU (SPORT) at expiry | Spores survive stomach transit and germinate in the small intestine within ~3 h (ileostomy study, n=11). A 4-week RCT in healthy adults found no significant change in GI symptoms, microbiota diversity or inflammatory markers; a small pilot found increased anti-inflammatory immune cell populations after LPS stimulation. A baseball trial found lower TNF-α but no performance or body-composition effect. **Verdict: safe, plausibly mildly immunomodulatory, not demonstrated to improve protein digestion** |
| **Protease, peptidase, aspergillopepsin, bromelain, papain** | Not disclosed (units not given) | In theory these hydrolyse protein. In practice, gastric acid inactivates most exogenous enzymes, and the powder is consumed with a meal or alone in 8–10 oz of water, spending minutes in the stomach. There is **no clinical evidence that the enzyme blend in this product increases amino acid absorption**. The claim "easily digested" is plausible for a plant isolate but not attributable to the enzymes |
| **Lactase** | Not disclosed | Irrelevant in a dairy-free product; included for marketing symmetry |
| **Lipase, cellulase, phytase, xylanase, hemicellulase, beta-glucanase, pectinase** | Not disclosed | Phytase could modestly improve mineral absorption if active; the rest are not clinically meaningful at undisclosed doses |

**Labeling problem:** enzyme activity is normally declared in units (HUT, SAPU, FCC). This label declares none, so the amounts cannot be compared with any studied dose.

## 3.6 "Weight loss" actives in Raw Organic Fit

| Ingredient | Amount | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Svetol (decaffeinated green coffee bean extract)** | Part of a 700 mg blend with ashwagandha | Small trials (n≈30–50) reported 4–8% weight loss over 8–12 weeks; later trials and reviews are mixed, effects are small, and the chlorogenic acid mechanism is unproven. The blend amount is not the same as the extract dose studied |
| **Organic ashwagandha** | Part of the same 700 mg | Some evidence for stress, sleep and cortisol; weak evidence for weight or fat loss. Generally safe at 300–600 mg/day; liver injury has been reported rarely |
| **Chromium (from brown rice chelate)** | 35–120 µg | Chromium picolinate has been studied for weight loss for decades with null-to-small results. 35 µg is below the studied doses (200–1000 µg), making a metabolic effect implausible |

**Verdict on Fit:** it is a high-protein, high-fiber powder with a small, underdosed "weight loss" blend. Its usefulness comes from 28 g protein and 9 g fiber per 170 kcal, not from Svetol, ashwagandha or chromium.

## 3.7 Ingredients that are absent (and the claims about them)

- **No artificial colors, flavors or sweeteners** — true; the label uses only organic/natural flavor and stevia/erythritol.
- **No soy ingredients** — true as an ingredient; the Creamy Oatmilk label nonetheless says "may contain traces of soy due to agricultural practices", and the facility handles soy.
- **No dairy** — true as an ingredient; facility handles milk.
- **No gluten** — certified NSF gluten-free at <10 ppm (not the FDA <20 ppm gluten-free claim). Oats are not an ingredient, but oatmilk powder in the Creamy line raises a cross-contact question the certification is designed to answer.
- **"No fillers or carriers"** — broadly true in the sense that there is no maltodextrin or silicone dioxide; but erythritol, gums, inulin and "natural flavors" are functional additives by any ordinary definition. The claim is a definitional trick, not a lie.
- **"Raw"** — no ingredient is heated above 115 °F according to the brand. See [04](04-protein-quality.md) for why this is not a nutritional advantage.

# 11. Comparisons against competing protein powders

## 11.1 The comparison problem

Protein powders are not tested on a common protocol. Consumer Reports tested 23 products in 2025 and 5 more in 2026; Clean Label Project tested 160–165; Labdoor tests sporadically; Mamavation tested 11 chocolate powders. **No product appears in every dataset with a full metal panel.** Any league table is therefore a collage, and this one says so explicitly.

Two rules for reading it:

1. **Compare within flavor and serving size.** Chocolate has more cadmium than vanilla; a 45 g serving carries more of everything than a 30 g serving.
2. **A missing result is not a pass.** "No public result" is different from "tested clean".

The within-brand contrast is the cleanest natural experiment in the whole dataset: **Garden of Life's own whey tested non-detect; its plant protein tested at 5.6× the CR level.** That is not a brand-quality problem. It is a plant-matrix problem, and it is the single strongest argument for choosing whey if you are not vegan.

Whey also wins on protein quality (PDCAAS 1.00, DIAAS 1.09–1.18), leucine (~100–110 mg/g vs 82–84 mg/g), price (~$0.04/g vs $0.084–0.093/g), and mixability. Its disadvantages are dairy allergy/lactose (largely removed in isolate), vegan incompatibility, and a less "whole food" story.

## 11.5 Against "clean label" competitors with no published data

Many premium brands (Nuzest, Ora, Naked, Puori, Ka'Chava, AG1-adjacent products) market on testing without publishing per-lot numbers. They cannot be ranked. The honest statement is: **absence of a published result is not evidence of cleanliness**, and brands that publish (Transparent Labs, Ritual, Momentous) deserve credit for doing so even when their numbers are not perfect.

## 11.6 What about taste, texture and mixability?

This report did not conduct a sensory panel. Third-party reviews consistently describe the Raw Organic line as **chalky/gritty with a strong stevia aftertaste**, the SPORT line as **better-mixing but salty**, and the Creamy Oatmilk line as **the best-tasting of the range**. Consumer reviews on Garden of Life's own site average ~4.4/5 for Raw Organic Protein, with recurring complaints about taste, clumping and container fill. Those are user impressions, not measurements; taste is the one dimension where the cheapest whey and the most expensive organic plant protein are genuinely different products, and it is a legitimate reason to choose one over the other.

## 11.7 Summary ranking for a health-motivated buyer

1. **Whey isolate (if not vegan):** best protein quality, lowest metals, lowest price.
2. **Low-lead plant protein with published data** (Truvani, Ritual, KOS, Transparent Labs): better contaminant profile than GOL at similar or lower cost.
3. **GOL Raw Organic Meal** (if you want a high-fiber meal replacement): cheapest per gram, but Labdoor metal fail and no per-SKU transparency.
4. **GOL SPORT** (only if NSF Certified for Sport is a hard requirement): best GOL protein quality, worst GOL lead result.
5. **GOL Raw Organic Protein / Protein & Greens / Creamy Oatmilk:** hardest to justify, premium price, no published metal numbers (or bad ones), mid-tier protein quality.
